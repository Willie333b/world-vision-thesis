**Abstract**

In the context of global efforts toward educational equity, World Vision Taiwan has provided various kinds of differentiated support to disadvantaged students, including access to the PaGamO learning platform for selected individuals. This study examines PaGamO’s effect on psychosocial and academic outcomes among 1,641 student recipients of PaGamO and a comparison group of similar students in the student databases of World Vision Taiwan. Using Propensity Score Matching (PSM) to reduce selection bias and a Lagged Dependent Variable (LDV) model to account for prior performance, we assess changes in psychological capital, student learning actions, and demands of educational support. Results indicate that PaGamO usage is associated with increased student dignity and willingness to self-learn, but not changes in academic performance. Additionally, weaker PaGamO performance predicts higher risk of failing at least one of Chinese, English, or mathematics, suggesting the platform’s potential as a screening tool for identifying students at risk of academic difficulty. These findings support PaGamO’s positive role in advancing World Vision Taiwan’s educational mission and offer implications for scaling similar interventions in resource-limited settings.

**Keywords**: rural education, game-based learning, competency-based learning, propensity score matching, lagged dependent variable model, psychological capital, differentiated support
